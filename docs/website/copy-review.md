# AtomsAI website copy review

**Source:** Figma file `pLyUMYhdCUYzwz0UXPtGY8`, page "Library · Site Copy" (`6:2`), section "Website Copy". 49 page frames plus 2 legal pages, read on 20 October 2026. The Figma file was only read, never edited.
**Standard:** The seven copy-editing sweeps (clarity, voice, so what, prove it, specificity, emotion, zero risk), weighted toward clarity, voice, so-what, prove-it and specificity. The copy is measured against the site's own `/brand` rules and the brand voice: senior, practical, precise, accountable, no AI hype, no invented results, sentence-case headlines with punctuation, and the name written **AtomsAI**.
**Counts:** Taken from the live Figma text across the 49 non-legal frames, about 13,500 words. Each figure is a count of occurrences and the number of pages they appear on.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The copy is careful and honest. It labels demos, illustrative workflows and third-party results more carefully than most B2B sites, and that should be protected. Its main weakness is abstraction. "Explore" appears 168 times on 47 of 49 pages, "connect" 118 times, "capability" 80, "the right" 57 and "starting point" 40. Meanwhile JustCall, Helpwise, ServiceAgent, CallPage and PyAI are rarely named where they would make a claim concrete. The homepage H1, "Powerful AI. Built for real use.", is the weakest headline on the site. It is the kind of AI hype the brand voice rules out, and it doesn't say who the site is for or what they get. The line under it, "Use a product. Build with our technology. Work with our team.", already does that job. Calls to action are the easiest problem to fix. One action, talking to AI Services, has at least 17 different labels. Only one of 17 infrastructure buttons uses the brand's "Explore the technology", and the approach page is linked under six different names. The central company claim, that each part informs the others, is stated on at least eight pages but never shown with a real example. Area names drift: Models & Infrastructure is written six ways and AI Services seven. The 14 agent pages and 6 industry pages share 57–59% of their text layers, so the template shows. Varying four slots per page fixes most of that. The legal pages need a legal pass more than a copy pass: they give conflicting privacy contact addresses and contain several outright errors.

## 2. Top 10 sitewide issues, ranked

### 1. Headlines describe an activity, not an offer, and the homepage H1 is hype

**Why it matters.** The H1 is the one line every visitor reads. On about 50 pages the H1 is a generic imperative: "Give…", "Keep…", "Make…", "Turn…", "Build…", "Help…", "Connect…". It tells readers what to do rather than what AtomsAI offers them. Few H1s name a product, a reader or an outcome. The homepage H1 opens with "Powerful", an unproven adjective that the brand voice ("no AI hype") rules out. Near-duplicate H1s blur pages together:
- "Turn … into … your team can use." appears on `/agents/document-intake` and `/agents/intelligence`.
- "a clearer starting point" appears on `/industries/insurance` and in the Kestrel example.

**Where.** `/`, `/applications`, `/infrastructure`, `/services`, `/capabilities`, `/capabilities/voice`, `/resources`, `/resources/case-studies`, `/trust`, most `/agents/*` and some `/industries/*`.

**Fix.** Each H1 should name the thing and who it is for. The lead then says what the reader gets. Keep the brand pattern of sentence case with a full stop.
- `/infrastructure`: "Build on the capabilities behind the experience." → "Speech and conversation technology you can build on."
- `/agents/crm`: "Keep the record closer to the work." → "CRM updates drafted from your calls, emails and meetings."

### 2. CTA labels break the invitation rule and multiply for the same destination

**Why it matters.** The brand rule is clear: "The invitation should lead to what the label promises." When the same action has many labels, readers can't tell whether two buttons go to the same place. Analytics also get fragmented. About 135 button and link labels begin with "Explore", so the word no longer signals the Applications and Infrastructure invitations it is reserved for.

**Where.**
- **AI Services enquiry:** at least 17 distinct labels, or 22 counting each industry variant. They include "Discuss a custom solution", "Tell us what you want to build", "Discuss your requirements" (5), "Discuss your project" (2), "Talk through your requirements", "Discuss a solution", "Discuss your voice experience", "Plan a custom voice workflow", "Talk through your use case", "Discuss your chat experience", "Plan your chat workflow", "Discuss this workflow" (14), "Plan this workflow" (14), "Discuss your workflow" (4), "Talk to the services team", "Discuss your <industry> workflow" (6), "Discuss your use case", "Help me choose" and "Talk to our team".
- **Infrastructure:** "Explore models & infrastructure" (10), "Explore infrastructure" (4), "Explore PyAI" (4), "Explore the underlying technology", "Explore technical capabilities", "See the PyAI stack" and "Visit PyAI". Only one button uses "Explore the technology".
- **Approach page:** "How we deliver" (14), "How we work" (6), "Explore our approach" (5), "See how we work", "See our approach" and "See the delivery approach".
- **Company:** "Talk to AtomsAI" (8), "Contact AtomsAI" and "Talk to our team".

**Fix.** Use one label per destination (see table 5.2). Reserve "Explore" for the Applications and Infrastructure invitations. Use "Try" for demos, "See" or "Read" for content, and "Discuss your requirements" for every AI Services conversation.
- Home hero: "Explore applications / Explore models & infrastructure / Discuss a custom solution" → "Explore the products / Explore the technology / Discuss your requirements"
- Agent pages, bottom: "Plan this workflow / How we deliver / Explore a related workflow" → "Discuss your requirements / See how we work / Explore quote agents" (name the actual related agent)

### 3. The central claim is asserted everywhere and shown nowhere

**Why it matters.** "Each part informs the others" is the company's differentiator. It is restated on `/`, `/applications`, `/infrastructure`, `/services`, `/company`, `/company/about`, `/brand` and `/resources/blog` ("Each part makes the others better", "Product experience shapes what we build next", "Custom work informs what comes next" and others). The site never gives one concrete instance, such as a problem seen in JustCall that led to a PyAI improvement. Repeated without evidence, the claim starts to sound like positioning rather than fact. The same gap shows on the agent and industry pages: only `/agents/intelligence` names a real product (JustCall, PyAI Trace). Everywhere else the reader gets "An existing application may cover part of the need."

**Where.** The eight pages above, the 14 `/agents/*` pages and the 6 `/industries/*` pages.

**Fix.** Ask the team for one real, publishable example of a capability moving between areas (needs proof). Use it once on `/` and once on `/company/about`, then cut the other restatements to a single sentence. On the agent and industry pages, name the relevant product or PyAI capability where the copy already supports it.
- `/` "Each part makes the others better" body 4: "That is how the pieces connect: practical needs shape what we build, and what we build expands what becomes possible." → "For example: [real case: an application need that led to a PyAI improvement — needs proof]."
- `/agents/sales` foundations: "An existing application may cover part of the need. Model and infrastructure capabilities provide another starting point." → "If your team already calls and messages through JustCall or takes website callbacks through CallPage, start there. We build the qualification and routing around it. [Confirm which steps the products cover natively.]"

### 4. Abstract nouns and filler words carry the copy

**Why it matters.** The brand asks for "specific examples". The copy leans on the same few abstractions, which makes pages hard to tell apart and easy to skim past. Sentence length is fine: the longest sentence is 28 words. The problem is vocabulary, not grammar.

**Where (count / pages).**
| Word | Count | Pages |
|---|---|---|
| explore | 168 | 47 |
| work | 145 | 48 |
| build | 130 | 41 |
| connect | 118 | 43 |
| capability | 80 | 45 |
| help | 72 | 37 |
| the right | 57 | 38 |
| may | 51 | 33 |
| experience | 44 | 23 |
| starting point | 40 | 34 |
| useful | 33 | 22 |
| next step | 29 | 24 |
| combination | 29 | 19 |
| layer | 18 | 14 |

**Fix.** Treat these as flag words, not banned words. In each case, replace the word with the thing it stands for.
- "the right" → name the criterion ("the sales team that owns the region", "the reviewer with approval rights").
- "capabilities" → name the capability ("speech recognition", "document extraction", "retrieval").
- "useful" → say what it does.
- "starting point" → keep it once per page at most.
- "layer" → keep it in the company description. Elsewhere, say "model", "infrastructure", "application" or "integration".

Examples:
- `/` Applications card: "Products that make useful capabilities part of everyday work." → "Ready-to-use products for customer conversations: business calling and messaging, shared inboxes, home-service bookings and website callbacks."
- `/capabilities/voice`: "That technical depth lets us consider the whole experience: the model, the conversation flow and the action that follows." → "Because we build the speech models, we can tune the model as well as the script and the follow-up action."

### 5. The three areas are named inconsistently

**Why it matters.** `/brand` says "Name the three areas consistently." The site doesn't follow that, and readers can't tell whether "AtomsAI Services", "the services team" and "custom AI services" are one thing or three.

**Where.**
- **Models & Infrastructure:**
  - "Models & Infrastructure" appears only on `/brand`.
  - "Models & infrastructure" appears on `/`, `/company/press`, `/changelog`, the `/infrastructure` page title, and 10 button labels.
  - "Models and infrastructure" is used as a heading on `/company/careers`, `/help-center` and `/trust`.
  - "models and infrastructure" appears 10 times in prose, and "model and infrastructure" once on `/capabilities/voice`.
- **AI Services:**
  - "AI Services" appears 27 times.
  - "AtomsAI Services" appears 16 times (`/services`, all 14 agent pages and `/agents`).
  - "AI services" appears on `/company`, `/resources/case-studies`, `/company/contact` and `/company/press`.
  - "services team" appears 13 times.
  - "custom services" appears on `/industries` and `/testimonials`.
  - "custom AI services" appears on `/company` and `/company/press`.
  - "Custom solutions" is used as a heading on `/company/careers` and `/trust`.
- **The company name itself is clean.** There are no instances of "Atoms AI", "AtomsAi" or "ATOMSAI".

**Fix.** Treat area names as proper nouns: **Applications**, **Models & Infrastructure**, **AI Services**. Use them in headings, navigation, labels and the first prose mention on each page. After that, lowercase descriptions such as "our models" or "custom engineering" are fine. Refer to the people as "the AI Services team". Using the ampersand also removes the "models and infrastructure, and" stutter.
- `/company/contact`: "across our applications, models and infrastructure, and AI services." → "across Applications, Models & Infrastructure and AI Services."
- `/agents/sales`: "An example of what we can build through AtomsAI Services." → "An example of what we can build through AI Services."

### 6. Specific claims lack source, date or substance

**Why it matters.** The brand asks the copy to separate "a measured outcome from an ambition". Several claims are either unsourced or vaguer than they look.

**Where.**
- `/applications`: "JustCall reports 6,000+ teams and more than a billion conversations." There is no source link or as-of date, and the link text is "See JustCall." The follow-up, "product experience at meaningful scale", is self-assessment.
- `/company/about`: "years of building products" (how many years?).
- `/infrastructure`: PyAI is described only as "speech capabilities". There are no languages, access model or other facts a developer checks first.
- `/agents/intelligence`: "PyAI Trace provides an owned model capability". The product isn't introduced anywhere else, and "owned" is jargon.
- `/resources`, `/resources/case-studies`: "Success stories" is plural, but there is one story and it belongs to JustCall.
- `/testimonials`: "Customer perspectives" and "Hear from the people using the products" are plural, but there is one quote.
- AI Services has no evidence of delivered work anywhere on the site.
- `/legal/privacy`: "Our top priority…" and "industry-standard measures".

**Fix.** Add the source and date, or soften the claim. Where proof is missing, flag it as needing proof rather than filling the gap.
- `/applications`: "JustCall reports 6,000+ teams and more than a billion conversations. That is product experience at meaningful scale…" → "JustCall reports more than 6,000 teams and over a billion conversations [needs source link and as-of date]. Running products at that volume shows us where conversations break down."
- `/resources/case-studies` H1: "See the work in context." → "One customer story. Three example workflows."

### 7. Illustrative examples are labeled in the copy but framed as customer case studies

**Why it matters.** The disclaimers are good ("Illustrative workflow. This is not a customer case study…"). The surrounding signals undercut them:
- The URLs carry invented company names that read like customers: `kestrel-underwriting-intelligence`, `meridian-health-support-knowledge`, `northwind-invoice-compound-system`. These names may belong to real companies, so check them.
- The pages sit under `/resources/case-studies`.
- The section is called "Success stories & workflow examples".
- `/changelog` notes that "example customer stories and profiles" existed and were later relabeled.

URLs, link previews and search results show the slug and title without the disclaimer.

**Where.** `/resources`, `/resources/case-studies` and the three example pages.

**Fix.** Rename the route and slugs, and keep each disclaimer directly under the H1.
- `/resources/case-studies/kestrel-underwriting-intelligence` → `/resources/examples/underwriting-submission`. Do the same for `/support-knowledge` and `/invoice-intake`.
- Section name: "Success stories & workflow examples" → "Customer story & example workflows"

### 8. The agent and industry templates show through

**Why it matters.** On each agent page, 16 of 28 text layers (57%) are identical across all 14 pages, plus a shared closing sentence. On each industry page, 16 of 27 (59%) are identical across all six. Readers who compare two agents see the same five section headings, the same "existing application may cover part of the need" paragraph, the same scope paragraph and two near-identical CTA pairs. On the hubs, each card's body repeats that page's H1 word for word, so the cards don't say what the agent does. Generic, repeated link text is also an accessibility problem: screen-reader users who navigate by links hear "Explore the workflow" three times per industry page, and "Explore the example" and "Read the article" three times each.

**Where.** `/agents/*` (14), `/agents`, `/industries/*` (6) and `/industries`.

**Fix.** Keep the skeleton, which is sound, but vary the four slots that carry meaning: H1, foundations (name products or PyAI), example, and the related-agent CTA. Make the section H2s functional and short. Move the boilerplate scope and terms lines into one small shared footer component.
- Agent H2s: "Shape the workflow around your team / Build on the right foundations / Keep control of the important decisions / Evaluate the work that matters / Discuss the scope" → "What the agent does / What it connects to / What stays with your team / How we measure it / What shapes the project"
- Industry cards: "Explore the workflow" (×3) → "Explore patient access agents", "Explore knowledge agents", and so on.

### 9. Hedged, defensive phrasing dilutes an accountable voice

**Why it matters.** Accountability is a brand trait, and stating limits is right. Repeated hedges, though, start to read as reluctance:
- "may" appears 51 times on 33 pages.
- "approved", "permitted", "agreed" and "authorized" together appear about 100 times.
- "can help" and "we will help" appear 24 times.

Examples include "An application may solve most of the problem. Your systems… may call for something more", "use approved product information", "permitted order information" and "authorized billing records". The controls get stated again in every capability description, then a third time in the control section.

**Where.** Sitewide, heaviest on `/agents/*`, `/industries/*`, `/applications`, `/company/about` and `/approach`.

**Fix.** State controls once per page, in the control section, and in plain words. Elsewhere, describe the action directly. Replace "we will help" with what you will actually do.
- `/agents/support`: "Use approved help content and relevant account records to respond to the question." → "Answer from your help content and the customer's account records."
- `/` closing: "We will help you find the right starting point across our applications, infrastructure and services." → "We will recommend whether a product, our technology or a custom build fits best."

### 10. Mechanics: spelling, casing, link names and legal errors

**Why it matters.** Each of these is small, but together they erode the "precise" part of the voice. The two contact addresses are a trust problem.

**Where.**
- **US spelling** is dominant (recognize, organize, authorize, summarize, behavior, judgment, labeled, center, afterward). The exceptions are "enquiry/enquiries" (17 uses on 13 pages, including the `/brand` rule itself) and "catalogue" (3).
- **Hub label casing bug:** "Explore crm agents" on `/agents`.
- **"Trust Center"** (`/help-center`, legal related links) vs "Trust & security" (`/trust` title) vs "trust & security" (`/capabilities` label).
- **H2 punctuation is mixed.** Multi-sentence H2s end with a full stop ("One company. Connected work."); single-phrase H2s don't.
- **A straight apostrophe** in "customer's" on `/company/about`. Curly apostrophes are used everywhere else.
- **Privacy contact address conflict.** `/legal/privacy` uses start@atomsai.com for privacy requests, while `/help-center` and `/trust` say privacy@atomsai.com.
- **Errors in the legal copy**, listed under the legal pages in section 4.

**Fix.** Adopt US English throughout (inquiry, catalog), set the style choices in table 5.3, fix the label generator to keep acronyms, pick one name for the trust page, and route both legal pages through counsel.
- `/agents/front-desk`: "Give every enquiry a clear place to go." → "Give every inquiry a clear place to go."
- `/help-center`: "Read the Trust Center" → "Read Trust & security" (or rename the page "Trust Center" everywhere)

### Three alternative hero pairs

All three use the brand description and invent nothing.

**A. Three ways in (recommended)**
- **H1:** Use our products. Build on our models. Or build with us.
- **Lead:** AtomsAI builds AI models and infrastructure, develops applications such as JustCall and Helpwise, and works with businesses on custom solutions. Each part informs the others, so we can build at the layer your problem requires.
- **CTAs:** Explore the products · Explore the technology · Discuss your requirements

**B. Model to product**
- **H1:** From speech models to the products your team uses.
- **Lead:** Through PyAI, we build speech models and infrastructure. We develop applications including JustCall, Helpwise, ServiceAgent and CallPage. Through AI Services, we build what a product cannot cover. Each part informs the others.

**C. Problem first**
- **H1:** Some problems need a product. Some need a new model or a custom system. We build all three.
- **Lead:** AtomsAI builds AI models and infrastructure, develops applications and works with businesses on custom solutions. We check what an existing product covers before proposing custom engineering.

**Recommendation:** A. It promotes the page's best existing line, says what the reader gets, maps one-to-one onto the three brand invitations and stays clear of hype. C has more personality and is worth an A/B test once the CTAs are fixed.
