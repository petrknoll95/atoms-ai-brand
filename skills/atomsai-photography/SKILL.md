# AtomsAI photography

## The idea: in their element

Make the person feel like someone with a life, a point of view and a place in the world. Give the viewer a reason to look: an arresting expression, a relationship, movement through a space, or a detail that reveals character. A photograph contributes human presence and specificity. Product UI explains the product; diagrams explain the system. Photography need not demonstrate someone using software.

AtomsAI spans applications, models and services. Do not reduce every assignment to an operator at a laptop. The subject may be a customer, a colleague, an encounter or a place that matters to their story. Establish the connection from the supplied assignment. Do not invent a customer outcome, company location or employee biography to justify an attractive scene.

This standalone Markdown file can be attached in ChatGPT or installed as a skill. Use the image capability available in the conversation. If asked for images, render images and inspect them; if asked for prompts, supply prompts. Do not require a particular vendor, account or script. If image generation is unavailable, say so and supply a usable prompt.

## Find the photograph before writing the prompt

Read the brief for subject, purpose, required action, references, format and placement. Preserve those choices. An empty room remains empty; a requested night scene stays at night. The house direction supports an assignment rather than replacing it with one of the examples below.

Decide what this particular image lets the viewer notice. Choose one dominant photographic device that serves it: proximity for listening, scale for a person's relationship to a place, a pause for character, or movement for an encounter. Change subject, camera distance or timing when an image feels generic; adding adjectives such as “premium,” “candid” or “authentic” will not supply an idea.

Proceed when the brief is open. Ask only for a missing fact or reference essential to the requested result. Research a real story when evidence is supplied or requested; do not turn an ordinary image request into a discovery project. Treat invented test scenes as concepts, never as evidence that an event happened.

## Photographic language

- **Character.** Use individual faces, normal asymmetry, personal clothing and a legible state of mind. Attention, humor, hesitation and resolve are useful. People need not smile. A deliberate portrait can include direct eye contact and a relaxed pose; a candid interaction should continue without awareness of the camera. Do not disguise an obviously directed portrait as reportage.
- **Composition.** Make a confident choice about distance, height, geometry and what falls outside the frame. Use architecture, foregrounds or negative space when they strengthen the subject. A close face and a distant figure can belong to the same series. Avoid repeating the centered waist-up person or the over-shoulder laptop angle.
- **Light.** Let the place and time explain it. Directional daylight, open shade, reflected light, an overcast exterior and believable night light are all available. Shadows can be graphic; faces should retain readable detail. Do not make every image soft, flat, beige or golden. Natural light need not mean visually timid light.
- **Color.** Keep local color and believable skin. Let a blue shirt, red wall or green landscape remain itself. Use rich but credible color separation, normal highlight roll-off and photographic texture. Avoid a uniform amber wash, teal shadows, beauty smoothing, HDR detail or artificial film damage.
- **Place.** Find specificity in proportion, materials, geography and signs of life. Well-made, maintained spaces can feel substantial without luxury props. Use outdoors, thresholds, shared places or personal settings when the story supports them. Do not manufacture grime for authenticity, or fill every scene with pale timber, plants and expensive office furniture. Preserve a real supplied location's character.
- **Behavior.** Describe a situation and a moment, leaving room for ordinary behavior. Do not choreograph every finger, gaze and prop. Candid does not mean random clutter or deliberate awkwardness. Movement should have believable balance, contact and direction.
- **Finish.** Keep natural skin, hair, fabrics and surface detail. Check hands, faces, glasses, equipment and reflections. Heavy texture and ornate micro-patterns are generation defects, not photographic richness.

Brand colors are Deep green #1D3C34, Cream #F6F1E8, Forest green #43695B and Stone #BAC5B9. They belong chiefly in the surrounding layout. Use them in a scene only where appropriate to the brief; never apply a green cast to skin or recolor an entire location. Logos, type, patterns and glass effects stay off the photograph unless the assignment explicitly requires them.

## Adapt to the use

| Use | What photography adds | Useful treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer story | The person whose decisions and experience give the story weight. | An environmental portrait, a meaningful encounter or a revealing detail from their real world. Choose the moment from the story rather than automatically illustrating software use. Actual testimonials require the actual subject. |
| Support | A sense of attention and a recognizable human presence. | A close listening moment or an honest portrait. A headset and a monitor are unnecessary visual shorthand. A named UI avatar must depict that person; identify automation with the existing brand system and a visible label. |
| Onboarding | Welcome and the beginning of a relationship. | Arrival, orientation or a conversation in context when an editorial image is useful. Use UI and diagrams for procedural steps. Do not decorate every instruction with a face. |
| Careers | The particular people and relationships that give a team character. | Colleagues between tasks, an individual portrait or an interest outside work supplied by the profile. Real engineering work is also valid when the story calls for it. Do not invent a perk, activity, headquarters or lifestyle and present it as employment evidence. |

These are roles for images, not four compulsory scenes. A photograph is optional when it contributes nothing beyond the surrounding words or UI.

## Compose for placement

Honor ratio, focal position and reserved copy space. Keep the meaningful subject together through the intended crop. Leave headroom for a full-head portrait unless the brief calls for a deliberately tight crop. Do not mirror people or environments simply to fit a layout.

The site's in-use specimen uses a 16:9 frame with a separate 40% left copy panel and a 60% right photograph. Its mobile version stacks copy above a deliberate 4:5 crop. This is one layout, not a rule to leave 40% of every photograph empty. Keep logos and essential copy outside the image; follow the site's placement and clear-space rules when producing a designed composition.

For a series, assign each image a different job. Review the set for distance, setting, activity, light and emotional tone. Vary the things that carry meaning; changing clothing or casting around the same desk is not sufficient. Keep a coherent approach to color and human detail without making all locations look identical. Do not add sports, hobbies or travel merely to escape office imagery—the supplied story must warrant them.

## Render and edit

Translate the idea into a short, concrete generation instruction: subject and moment; specific place; shot scale and viewpoint; light; format and crop needs; the few exclusions that matter. If the assignment depends on a transition such as arrival, make the destination and direction visible; walking near a doorway does not establish entering it. Avoid long lists of props and contradictory camera directions. Include no unresolved placeholders.

Use references according to their role. Preserve identity and required objects for an edit; a treatment reference need not dictate its setting or composition. Do not invent a real person's likeness. Use separate generations for separate requested assets. Produce a contact sheet only when asked for one.

Inspect the rendered pixels, not only the prompt. First ask whether the photograph has a clear subject, a believable moment and a reason to keep looking. Then examine anatomy, materials, light, reflections and the requested crop. Check images together and in the actual layout where available. If the central idea fails, change the photographic decision. If a local defect fails, make a targeted correction. Stop iterating on a source that accumulates artificial texture.

Return the images or requested files with minimal text. Keep production notes and synthetic provenance in the accompanying record, outside the photograph. Do not add unsolicited warning panels, captions, checklists, mock quotations or publication claims.

## Test the skill on outcomes

For a requested evaluation, set observable acceptance criteria before generation, render a small set of relevant assignments, and save the exact prompts and outputs. Include an unfamiliar assignment for another agent to execute from this file without supplying its intended answer. Inspect and report actual visual failures as well as successes. A subagent can test instruction transfer and find visible defects; it cannot establish customer recognition or independent audience preference. Do not claim those validations unless they occurred.
